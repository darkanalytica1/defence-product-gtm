# Multi-domain operations, in practical terms

You will hear "multi-domain" constantly. It is worth understanding properly,
because it changes what your product has to do, not just how it is described.

## The plain version

The traditional domains are land, sea and air. Space and cyberspace are now
routinely treated alongside them. Some frameworks add the information or
cognitive dimension, on the argument that perception and belief are contested
in their own right.

The older way of working was that each domain planned and fought largely on
its own, coordinating at the edges. The multi-domain idea is that effects
should be combined across domains deliberately and quickly, so that activity
in one creates an advantage in another.

That is the concept. It is not new as an aspiration. What is new is the
ambition to do it at speed, routinely, and at lower levels of command rather
than only at the very top.

## Why it is hard, and therefore why there is a market

The difficulty is almost entirely organisational and informational rather
than technical in the narrow sense.

- **The domains have different clocks.** Things happen at very different
  speeds in cyberspace and in a ground manoeuvre. Synchronising activity
  across those tempos is genuinely difficult.
- **They have different languages.** Each community has its own vocabulary,
  data formats, planning processes and ways of describing a situation. The
  same object may have three names and three identifiers.
- **They have different authorities.** Who may authorise an effect differs by
  domain, and some require approval at very senior levels.
- **They have different classifications.** Information that is freely shared
  in one domain may be tightly controlled in another, which makes a single
  common picture legally as well as technically hard.
- **They were bought separately.** Systems were procured by different
  programmes, at different times, against different requirements, from
  different suppliers.

Every one of those is a product opportunity and a product constraint at the
same time.

## What it means for your product

**Your data model is now a diplomatic problem.** The moment your product
handles information from more than one domain, you inherit the question of
how those communities describe the world, and you will not get to pick a
favourite. Products that impose their own ontology on everybody tend to be
adopted by nobody.

**Translation is a feature, not plumbing.** The ability to ingest something
in one community's format and express it in another's, without silently
losing meaning, is often the actual value being bought. Treat it as a first
class part of the product with its own quality bar, not as an adapter layer
somebody writes at the end.

**Provenance becomes essential.** When information crosses domains and
classification boundaries, the question "where did this come from and how
confident are we" stops being good practice and becomes a precondition for
anyone acting on it. A system that merges sources into a clean picture and
discards where each part came from has destroyed the thing that made it
usable.

**Partial participation is the normal case.** Assume some participants will
have full connectivity and rich data, and others will have almost nothing.
The product must be useful at both ends of that range. Designing for the well
connected case and treating the degraded case as an edge condition is a
common and expensive mistake, because the degraded case is what the
capability was bought for.

**Timeliness has to be visible.** If your product shows a picture built from
sources of very different ages, showing the age is as important as showing
the picture. A confident display of stale information is worse than an honest
gap, because someone will act on it.

## The honest summary

Multi-domain is not a technology. It is an integration and coordination
problem with a technology component. The suppliers who do well are the ones
who understand that, and whose products make somebody else's integration
easier rather than adding another island to be integrated.
