# Reading list

Public, open access material worth the time of a commercial or product person
entering this sector. Everything listed here is published openly by the
organisation concerned. Nothing in this guide is derived from any of it: these
are pointers for your own reading, not sources this text was assembled from.

Availability and web addresses change. If a link has moved, search for the
title.

## Command and control

The NATO accredited Command and Control Centre of Excellence publishes
openly on command and control at the operational level, including its three
standing areas of interest: C2 processes and structures, information and
knowledge management, and human factors.

- Centre site: <https://c2coe.org/>
- *Annals of Command and Control*, its peer reviewed open access journal,
  produced with the Canadian Joint Warfare Centre: <https://c2coe.org/annals/>

Recent volumes have addressed multi-domain command and control, decision
tempo, trust in command relationships, and leadership development across
domains. For a product person, the human factors and trust material is
usually more immediately applicable than the doctrinal material.

## Alliance transformation and concepts

NATO's Allied Command Transformation publishes on future operating concepts,
multi-domain operations and the work of the centres of excellence.

- <https://www.act.nato.int/>

## National doctrine and defence reviews

Most European countries publish their defence strategy, capability plans and
much of their doctrine openly. These documents are the closest thing
available to a forward view of demand, and they are read by remarkably few
commercial teams. Start with your target customer's most recent published
defence review and its current capability or equipment plan.

## Procurement and opportunity sources

- Tenders Electronic Daily, the EU public procurement notice service:
  <https://ted.europa.eu/>
- The NATO Support and Procurement Agency publishes supplier information and
  business opportunities: <https://www.nspa.nato.int/>
- The European Defence Agency publishes on capability priorities and
  collaborative projects: <https://eda.europa.eu/>

## Standards and interoperability

Alliance standardisation documents, and the interoperability test events run
around them, are the practical reference for anyone whose product must
exchange information with others. Identify the specific standards and
versions your target customer uses, rather than relying on general lists.

## Human factors and decision making

The general literature on decision making under uncertainty and time pressure
transfers well, and is largely written outside defence. Work on naturalistic
decision making, on how experts actually decide under pressure rather than
how normative models say they should, is directly relevant to anyone building
decision support.

## Adjacent civilian domains

Air traffic management, maritime coordination, emergency management and
critical infrastructure operations all publish openly and share structural
features with military command and control: time pressure, incomplete
information, distributed authority, and severe consequences. Access to
practitioners is far easier and the transfer of insight is better than most
people expect.

## A note on how to read all of this

Read for the structure of the problem rather than for facts to repeat.
Vocabulary picked up and redeployed without understanding is transparent to
anyone from the sector, and it damages credibility more than plain language
ever would. The aim is to understand what these organisations are actually
trying to do, well enough to describe it in your own words.
